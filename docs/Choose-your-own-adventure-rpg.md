RimWorld’s Native Architecture: ThinkTrees & JobGiversInstead of calculating complex future plans like a GOAP system, RimWorld pawns constantly evaluate a rigid, top-down hierarchy of needs and priorities:ThinkTrees (Behavior Trees): A pawn's brain runs through an XML-defined tree from top to bottom. The top of the tree handles emergency checks (e.g., Am I on fire? Am I bleeding out? Is a raider actively shooting me?). If any of these are true, the pawn acts immediately.The Work Priority Matrix: If no emergencies exist, the tree drops down into the player-defined Work Tab. It evaluates tasks from left to right (e.g., Firefight → Patient → Doctor → Bed Rest) based on assigned numerical priorities (1 through 4).JobGivers: Once a valid work type is found, a JobGiver scans the immediate map area for a matching task (e.g., JobGiver\_Work finds a dirty tile to clean or a wall blueprint to build).2. Why RimWorld Uses ThinkTrees Instead of GOAPWhile GOAP is great for making NPCs feel highly intelligent and capable of adaptive problem-solving (like the squad tactics in F.E.A.R.), it has major drawbacks for a colony simulator:Performance Costs: GOAP requires a pathfinding algorithm (like A\*) over actions, calculating cost-benefit paths for every agent. Running GOAP calculations for 50 colonists, 100 farm animals, and a 60-pawn tribal raid simultaneously would cause severe late-game simulation lag.Player Agency: In a colony sim, the player wants predictable control. If you set a colonist to "Construct," you want them to look for construction blueprints immediately. A GOAP AI might decide on its own that it's more optimal to go brew beer first to satisfy a long-term happiness goal, which would frustrate the player.3.
